Overview

This comprehensive review session focuses on fundamental nucleophilic substitution pathways. We will dive deep into SN1 (substitution nucleophilic unimolecular) and SN2 (substitution nucleophilic bimolecular) pathways, examining rate laws, step mechanisms, substrate preferences, stereochemical consequences, and key solvent considerations.

SN1 Mechanism: Substitution Nucleophilic Unimolecular

The SN1 reaction is a substitution nucleophilic unimolecular reaction. It follows a two-step mechanism. First, the leaving group departs, forming a stable intermediate carbocation. Second, the nucleophile attacks the carbocation to form the product.

Stereochemistry Note:

Because the carbocation intermediate is planar (sp² hybridized), the nucleophile can attack from either side with equal probability, leading to a racemic mixture.
